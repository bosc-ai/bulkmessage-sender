The problem is the phone, not the patients

Most clinic front desks lose their day to three calls: booking, rescheduling and "are my reports ready". None of them need a human. All of them are answered faster in a chat the patient can read between other things.

The commercial case is the no-show. An empty slot cannot be resold after the fact, and a reminder people actually read is the cheapest way to keep the calendar full. WhatsApp gets read; an SMS reminder frequently does not.

The flows that pay for themselves

Appointment confirmation and reminders

Confirmation at the time of booking, then a reminder ahead of the slot with the time, the location and, for a virtual consultation, the meeting link. Weflux generates a Google Meet link automatically for virtual bookings and includes it in the reminder, so the patient is not hunting through email at the appointment time.

Self-serve rescheduling

A reschedule button on the reminder turns a phone call into a tap. It also converts a would-be no-show into a filled slot on another day, which is the outcome you actually want.

Report and prescription delivery

A notification when reports are ready, with a secure link rather than the document itself in the thread. This is the single biggest reduction in inbound calls for most diagnostics setups.

Refill and follow-up reminders

Timed to the prescription length for refills, and to the clinical follow-up interval for reviews. Both improve adherence and both bring people back without a sales message.

Handling patient data properly

This is the part that deserves care, and the part where a generic playbook is not good enough.

  • Send notifications, not clinical content. "Your report is ready, view it here" belongs in WhatsApp. A diagnosis, a test result or a scan does not. Put the content behind an authenticated link.
  • Minimise what is in the message. The patient knows what they booked. The reminder does not need to name the procedure or the department.
  • Confirm the number belongs to the patient. Family members share phones. A number captured at reception is not automatically the right recipient for anything sensitive.
  • Restrict who can read a thread. Role-based access means reception staff and clinicians need not see the same things.
  • Honour deletion. Patients can ask for their data to be removed, and you should be able to do it for one contact without a project.

India's DPDP Act treats health data as sensitive personal data, and your obligations do not change because the channel is convenient. Weflux supports role-based access, encryption in transit and at rest, and per-contact deletion. What we cannot do is decide your clinical disclosure policy, and any vendor offering to is worth avoiding. See the security page for the controls.
