Copy these, change the wording to sound like you, and submit them. Replace the `{{1}}`-style variables with your own fields.

**Every example here follows the two rules that cause most rejections:** no template begins or ends with a variable, and no two variables sit next to each other. If you adapt these, keep that intact.

## Order and shipping (Utility)

**Order confirmed**
> Hi {{1}}, your order {{2}} is confirmed. We will let you know as soon as it ships. Order total: {{3}}.
> Buttons: Track order · Contact us

**Shipped**
> Hi {{1}}, good news, your order {{2}} has been dispatched and should arrive by {{3}}.
> Button: Track shipment

**Out for delivery**
> Hi {{1}}, your order {{2}} is out for delivery today. Please keep your phone reachable.
> Buttons: Reschedule · Contact us

**Delivered**
> Hi {{1}}, your order {{2}} was delivered today. We hope it is exactly what you wanted.
> Button: Rate your order

## Cash on delivery (Utility)

**COD confirmation**
> Hi {{1}}, please confirm your cash-on-delivery order {{2}} for {{3}} so we can dispatch it today.
> Buttons: Confirm order · Cancel

This one earns its place for Indian D2C. Every unconfirmed COD parcel that ships is a return-to-origin cost paid twice.

## Cart recovery (Marketing)

**Reminder**
> Hi {{1}}, you left {{2}} in your cart. It is still there if you would like to finish your order.
> Button: Complete order

**Reminder with an offer**
> Hi {{1}}, still thinking about {{2}}? Here is {{3}} off if you complete your order in the next 24 hours.
> Button: Complete order

Both are Marketing, correctly declared. Submitting either as Utility is the classic rejection.

## Appointments (Utility)

**Confirmation**
> Hi {{1}}, your appointment is confirmed for {{2}} at {{3}}. We will send a reminder the day before.
> Buttons: Reschedule · Cancel

**Reminder**
> Hi {{1}}, a reminder about your appointment tomorrow, {{2}} at {{3}}.
> Buttons: Confirm · Reschedule

Keep the procedure or department out of it. The person knows what they booked, and the message sits on a device that may be shared.

## Re-engagement and stock (Marketing)

**Back in stock**
> Hi {{1}}, {{2}} is back in stock. You asked us to let you know when it returned.
> Button: Shop now

The second sentence matters. Reminding someone they opted in measurably lowers the block rate.

**Win-back**
> Hi {{1}}, it has been a while. Here is {{2}} off your next order if you would like to come back.
> Button: Shop now · Footer: Reply STOP to opt out

## Payments (Utility)

**Payment reminder**
> Hi {{1}}, a reminder that your payment of {{2}} is due on {{3}}. You can pay using the link below.
> Button: Pay now

Keep it factual. A reminder that reads as pressure is both a conduct risk and a fast route to being blocked.

## Adapting these safely

**Start with text, always.** "Hi {{1}}" is fine; "{{1}}, your order" is not.

**Never end on a variable.** Put the link behind a button rather than trailing `{{3}}` at the end of the message.

**Separate your variables.** `{{1}} {{2}}` adjacent reads as a blank to a reviewer.

**Give realistic samples.** Submit "Priya" and "WF-10428", not "xxxx" and "test".

**Declare the category honestly.** If the message would make no sense had the customer never bought anything, it is Marketing.

**Add an opt-out footer to Marketing templates.** It costs a few recipients and protects your quality rating, which is worth more.
