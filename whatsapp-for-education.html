The group-chat problem

Most institutions already run on WhatsApp, just badly. Announcements go into parent groups where forty people reply "noted", important messages scroll away in an afternoon, and a teacher's personal number becomes the school helpline at 10pm.

The fix is not to leave WhatsApp, which parents will not do. It is to move from groups to one-to-one messaging from an institutional number, so announcements reach individuals, replies come to a shared inbox several staff can work, and nobody's personal phone is the support channel.

Admissions

Admission enquiry volume is seasonal and unforgiving: the same week brings hundreds of enquiries, and the ones that go unanswered for two days enrol elsewhere.

  • Instant acknowledgement on every enquiry, with the two or three qualifying questions that route it correctly.
  • Stage tracking so enquired, visited, applied and enrolled are visible rather than remembered.
  • Deadline reminders for incomplete applications, which recover more admissions than any amount of new lead spend.
  • Document collection in the thread, so parents are not asked to email a scan they will not email.

Fees

Fee reminders are the flow institutions most often run manually and most obviously should not. A schedule ahead of the due date, a payment link in the message, an automatic receipt on payment, and a separate escalation path for genuinely overdue accounts. The receipt matters more than it looks: it removes the "did it go through" call entirely.

Day-to-day communication

Attendance alerts, exam schedules, result notifications, holiday and closure announcements, transport delays. All Utility-shaped, all better as an individual message than a group post. Segment by class, section or batch so a parent receives what concerns their child rather than everything.

For EdTech specifically

Course platforms have a different problem: enrolment is easy and completion is not. Class reminders before a live session, nudges when a learner goes quiet for a week, and renewal messages timed to the actual course end rather than the billing date. Watch reply rate against opt-out rate closely here, because learner lists are easy to over-message and expensive to rebuild.

A note on messaging minors

Communicate with parents or guardians rather than students where the student is a minor, and keep consent records against the guardian. India's DPDP Act sets specific expectations around children's data, and the practical implication for most schools is straightforward: the number on file should be the guardian's, and marketing to minors is not something to experiment with.
