What is a WhatsApp broadcast?

A WhatsApp broadcast is a message sent to many recipients at once, where each person receives it as an individual one-to-one chat rather than a group. Recipients cannot see each other, and replies come back to you privately, into the same thread they would use for any other conversation.

That last part is what separates a broadcast from an email blast. A broadcast is not a one-way announcement. Anyone who receives it can reply, and when they do, a 24-hour window opens in which you can respond with ordinary free-form messages. Most of the commercial value of WhatsApp broadcasting sits in those replies, not in the send itself, which is why a broadcast tool without a decent inbox behind it tends to disappoint.

On the official WhatsApp Business API, a broadcast to people who have not messaged you recently must use a message template approved by Meta in advance. You cannot compose a fresh paragraph and blast it to 5,000 people. This constraint is the whole reason official broadcasting is sustainable: it is also why the numbers sending it do not get banned.

Official API or an unofficial bulk sender?

If you have searched for a WhatsApp bulk message sender, you have seen tools promising unlimited messages from your personal or Business App number, usually for a one-time fee. They work by automating the WhatsApp app itself, which violates WhatsApp's terms of service. Numbers using them get banned in waves, and a banned number is generally not recoverable.

How to send a WhatsApp broadcast

Once your number is connected, a campaign is five steps.

  1. Connect your WhatsApp Business number. Weflux uses Meta's Embedded Signup, so you authorise through Facebook Business Manager and come back with official API access. An existing number on the WhatsApp Business app, or with another provider, can be migrated across.
  2. Create and submit a template. Write the message, mark the variable parts (a name, an order number, a date), pick the right category, and submit it to Meta. Review is usually quick, though it is Meta's queue and not ours, so plan a day rather than a minute for anything time-sensitive.
  3. Choose who receives it. Import contacts by CSV or pull them from an integration, then narrow the list with tags, custom attributes or lead stage. Sending to everyone is almost always the wrong answer.
  4. Fill in the variables and preview. Map each template variable to a contact field. The preview renders exactly what a recipient sees, which is the cheapest possible place to catch a broken merge field.
  5. Send or schedule. Send immediately, or schedule for a sensible local hour. Weflux paces delivery to your account's messaging limit rather than firing everything at once and collecting failures.

Template categories, and which one to use

Meta sorts every template into a category, and the category determines both what you are allowed to say and what the message costs. Choosing the wrong one is the most common reason a template is rejected.

CategoryUse it forExamples
MarketingAnything promotional, or anything whose purpose is to bring someone backOffers, launches, cart recovery, re-engagement, newsletters
UtilityA message about a transaction or request the customer has already madeOrder confirmations, shipping updates, payment receipts, appointment reminders
AuthenticationOne-time passcodes and account verification, nothing elseLogin OTP, transaction verification

Marketing is the most expensive category and the most scrutinised. Utility is cheaper, but it only applies where a real transaction exists: dressing a promotion up as an order update gets the template rejected, and repeated attempts affect your account standing. Authentication templates have a fixed shape and cannot carry marketing copy.

Meta sets and periodically revises the per-message rates by category and destination country. Weflux passes those charges through at cost, so the current rate is whatever Meta publishes for India, and every invoice line shows it.

The 24-hour customer service window

When a customer sends you a message, a 24-hour window opens. Inside it you can reply with ordinary free-form messages: text, images, documents, whatever the conversation needs, with no template and no approval. When the window closes, you can only reach that person again with an approved template.

This single rule shapes how good WhatsApp teams work. It is why a shared inbox matters more than it looks like it should, and why response time is a commercial metric rather than a service nicety. A reply that lands nine hours late may still be inside the window. One that lands twenty-six hours late costs you a template message and a great deal of goodwill.

Who gets the broadcast

Weflux stores contacts with tags, custom attributes and a lead stage, and any combination of those can define an audience. In practice the segments that earn their keep are unglamorous: customers who bought in the last ninety days, leads who replied but never converted, people in one city, people who abandoned a cart this week.

Opt-outs are handled for you. When someone asks to stop, they are excluded from future campaigns automatically rather than left for someone to remember. That is both a compliance requirement and a quality-rating protection, since blocks and reports are what push a number's quality rating down.

What you can measure

Every campaign reports accepted, delivered, read, replied and opted-out, sourced from Meta's own delivery webhooks rather than inferred. Failures are itemised with the reason Meta gave, which matters because the common failure modes have different fixes: an invalid number is a data problem, a template pause is a content problem, and hitting your messaging limit is a pacing problem.

Read rates on WhatsApp are genuinely high compared with email, and it is tempting to stop there. Replies and opt-outs are the more useful pair. A campaign with a high read rate and a high opt-out rate is not a success, it is a warning about the next one.

Protecting your number: quality rating and messaging limits

Meta assigns every WhatsApp Business number a quality rating based on how recipients react, principally blocks and reports. It also assigns a messaging limit, the number of unique customers you can start conversations with in a rolling 24 hours. Limits step up as you send consistently to people who want to hear from you, and step down when they do not.

What keeps a number healthy is not clever tooling, it is restraint:

  • Message people who actually opted in, and be able to show when and how they did
  • Make the first line say who you are, so nobody has to guess before deciding to block
  • Send at a sane frequency, and let the segment justify the send rather than the calendar
  • Honour opt-outs immediately, including the informal ones people type as a reply
  • Watch the quality rating after every large campaign, not once a quarter

Weflux paces sends to your current limit and surfaces the quality rating in the dashboard, but no platform can protect a number from a bad list. The tool cannot want your customers' consent on your behalf.
